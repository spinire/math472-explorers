Fitting a line when both coordinates are noisy

The three pictures from the notes (Figures 6, 7 and 8), made live. Sections 1 and 2 share the same six points, so a point you move in one moves in the other.

1. Three ways to measure a gap

Each line makes its own kind of squared gap (red) as small as possible. Least squares measures gaps vertically, which trusts x; measuring horizontally trusts y; the perpendicular gap treats both coordinates alike. Drag a point in the left panel, or focus it with Tab and move it with the arrow keys. All lines pass through the mean of the data, marked +.

2. Turning the line

Pick any line through the mean, in the direction of a unit vector w. Each point pi splits into a part along the line and a perpendicular gap di, the two legs of a right triangle, so ‖pi‖2 = (pi·w)2 + di2. Added over all the points, the two parts always make the same total. Turn the line and watch the total split.

The total is ‖X‖F2, the sum of the squared lengths of the centered points, and it does not depend on the line. So the gaps are smallest exactly where ‖Xw‖2 is largest: at w = v1, where ‖Xw‖2 = σ12 and the gaps add up to σ22.

3. A noisy cloud, and the start of PCA

150 points scattered about the line y = x (dotted), with noise added to each coordinate. Set how noisy each coordinate is and compare the two fits.

v1 line least squares, y on x v1, v2 (length 2σi/√n)

The arrows have length 2σi/√n, two standard deviations of the data in that direction, and the ellipse has the same axes. The cloud is roughly an ellipse: v1 points along its long axis, v2 along its short one, and the share σ12/(σ12 + σ22) is how much of the spread lies along v1. That is principal component analysis in two dimensions.

Things to try

  • Equal noise. Leave both at 0.7. Least squares comes out too shallow; the v1 line stays close to slope 1.
  • Only y noisy. Set the noise in x to 0. Now x really is exact, and least squares is the right tool. Which way is the v1 line off?
  • Only x noisy. Set the noise in y to 0. Both fits are now off. The perpendicular fit is fair only when the two coordinates are about equally noisy.
  • Lots of noise. Raise both. What happens to the share of the spread along v1, and to the shape of the ellipse?
