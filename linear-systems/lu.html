LU, step by step

Gaussian elimination turns A into an upper-triangular U by subtracting multiples of one row from the rows below it. LU factorization just keeps those multipliers, and they fill in L. Step through it, then reuse the factors to solve as many systems as you like.

Part 2: Solve Ax = b with the factors

With PA = LU in hand, Ax = b becomes two triangular solves: Ly = Pb top to bottom (forward substitution), then Ux = y bottom to top (back substitution). Every row has exactly one new unknown, so there is no elimination left to do.

Part 3: Factor once, solve many

Every time you finish a solve in Part 2, it counts here. Count one flop for each +, −, ×, ÷.

For large n, factoring costs about ⅔n³ flops and each pair of triangular solves about 2n². See what LU costs for how that plays out when n is in the thousands.
