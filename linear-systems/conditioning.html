Norms and condition numbers

Pivoting fixes what the algorithm can break. It can’t fix a problem that is sensitive in itself, and the condition number measures that sensitivity before you compute anything. To define it, you first need a way to measure size.

  1. Vector norms: three ways to measure size
  2. Matrix norms: the largest stretch
  3. Condition number: two nearly parallel lines

Vector norms: three ways to measure size

Drag the point x (or focus it and use the arrow keys). Each norm draws its own “circle” through x: all the points of the same size as x in that norm.

1-norm: diamond 2-norm: circle ∞-norm: square

The 1-norm adds up every error, the 2-norm is ordinary distance, and the ∞-norm reports only the single worst coordinate, which is what an engineer means by a tolerance. They always satisfy ‖x‖∞ ≤ ‖x‖2 ≤ ‖x‖1. Drag x onto an axis to see when they tie.

Matrix norms: the largest stretch

An induced matrix norm answers “how much can A stretch a vector, at most?”: ‖A‖ = max ‖Ax‖ over all x with ‖x‖ = 1. Below, the unit ball of the chosen norm (faint) is mapped by A to the shape drawn in blue. The farthest point of the image, measured in the same norm, is the matrix norm.

The 1- and ∞-norms have formulas you can read off the entries: the largest absolute column sum and the largest absolute row sum. The 2-norm has no such shortcut. The circle maps to an ellipse, and ‖A‖2 is its longest semi-axis, the largest singular value. You’ll meet that ellipse again with the SVD.

Condition number: two nearly parallel lines

Each equation of a 2×2 system is a line, and the solution is where they cross. When the lines are nearly parallel, a tiny nudge to one of them slides the crossing point a long way. The condition number, cond(A) = ‖A‖‖A−1‖, measures exactly this: it bounds how much a relative change in b can be amplified in x.

original solution (δ = 0)after the nudge

The bound ‖δx‖/‖x‖ ≤ cond(A) ‖δb‖/‖b‖ is a worst case over all directions of δb. A particular nudge can come close to it or stay well under it. Rule of thumb: doubles carry about 16 digits, and just storing b perturbs it at the 10−16 level, so cond(A) ≈ 10k means expect to lose about k of those 16 digits, however carefully you compute.
